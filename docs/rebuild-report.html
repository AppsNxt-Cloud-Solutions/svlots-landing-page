The short version

Three things were wrong that cost you business

Everything else in this document is detail. These three were the expensive ones, and all three are now fixed and verified.

The enquiry form never worked

The contact form sent submissions to https://localhost:7192 — an address that only exists on a developer's own laptop. We then checked your live server and found the receiving endpoint had never been deployed at all. Worse, when the send failed the form still told the visitor “Data Added Successfully.”

Every enquiry through that form was lost, and every visitor was told it had gone through.

Enquiries are delivered, or you are told

Submissions now go to an endpoint we confirmed is live on your server, and can also be emailed to you in parallel. The visitor sees success only if a delivery actually succeeded.

If both routes fail, they get an honest message plus your phone, WhatsApp and email — so the enquiry still reaches you.

Your cloud keys were public

The old site fetched your Amazon storage password into the visitor's browser and unscrambled it there — using a code that was itself written into the page. Anyone who opened their browser's developer tools could read your live credentials and access the storage account directly.

Credentials never leave the server

Images are now fetched by our server and passed to the browser. We measured the page afterwards: zero requests to Amazon and zero credentials anywhere in what the browser receives.

Search engines saw one blank page

All 15 pages shared a single title, SVlots. There was no page description, no sitemap, no business listing data and no analytics — so there was no way to know whether the site was working, and little for Google to rank.

Every page is findable

Each page now has its own title and description, a sitemap that updates itself when you add a project, and machine-readable business details — your Tumkur address, phone, opening hours and service list. Google scores the site 100 for SEO.

Security

What we changed, and how we proved it

Security claims are only worth as much as the test behind them. Each of these was checked by attempting the attack.

  • Cloud credentials. Measured the finished page: zero requests to Amazon from the browser, and no credential text anywhere in what the browser receives.
  • Image access. Our image route only serves files that belong to a published project. We tried an unlisted filename — refused. We tried a directory-escape trick (../etc/passwd) — refused.
  • Staff login. The session is a cryptographically signed token the browser cannot read or alter. We tried an expired token, a token signed with the wrong key, and a hand-forged unsigned token. All three were rejected.
  • Staff pages. Protected in three independent places, so a mistake in one does not open the door. Visiting the admin page without signing in redirects to the login screen.
  • File uploads. Restricted to JPEG, PNG and WebP, capped at 6 MB, and stored under a name derived from the file's own contents — so two people uploading plot.jpg can no longer overwrite each other's work, which the old site allowed.
  • Spam. Public forms now carry two automated-submission traps. The old forms had none.
  • Transport. Standard protective headers are now sent with every page, and the software version is no longer advertised.

One honest caveat. The new site no longer exposes your Amazon keys — but those keys were public for as long as the old site was online, so they must be treated as compromised regardless of anything we changed. Rotating them is listed in the actions below, and the new site is already built to use replacements the moment you issue them.

See it for yourself

Eight things worth demonstrating

Each takes under a minute. Numbers here are a suggested order for a walkthrough — the first few are what a customer notices, the last few are what protects you.

The homepage, scrolled slowly

Open the homepage and scroll from top to bottom without rushing.

What to point out: the headline settles word by word, the landscape drifts behind the text as you scroll, the four promises draw their own icons, and the statistics count up as they arrive. The old site's homepage was a slideshow that repeated one of its three photos.

A filtered project list that can be shared

Go to Projects, set Location to Bengaluru, then copy the web address from the bar and paste it into a new tab.

What to point out: the filter is part of the address, so the filtered view opens exactly as you left it. You can send a customer a link to precisely the properties they asked about. On the old site filters vanished the moment you moved away, and the back button did nothing.

The area calculator drawing your plot

Open Property Models → Area Calculator, choose Quadrilateral, and enter four sides of 10 with a diagonal of 14.142.

What to point out: the plot draws itself to scale as you type, labelled with your own measurements — and the answer appears at once in square metres, square feet, guntha and acres. Guntha and acres are what buyers actually ask for, and the old calculator could not produce them. Now change the diagonal to 99: instead of the old silent “0.00”, it explains that those lengths cannot close into a shape.

Tap to call, from anywhere

Open the site on a phone and look at the bar fixed to the bottom.

What to point out: Call, WhatsApp and Enquire follow the visitor down every page. The old site had no phone number on it at all.

A link that looks professional when shared

Paste a link to one of the Insights articles into WhatsApp or LinkedIn and wait for the preview to load.

What to point out: a branded card appears with the article headline, generated automatically. Old links showed as bare text, which quietly undermines everything your marketing team shares.

Publishing a project without a developer

Sign in at /login, add a project with a photo, then open the public Projects page.

What to point out: it appears immediately, and it is added to the sitemap Google reads — without anyone touching the code.

The locked door

While signed out, type the staff address directly into the browser: /admin/projects.

What to point out: you are sent to the login screen. On the old site this page opened for anybody who knew the address.

Nothing leaking to Amazon

On the Projects page, open your browser's developer tools, choose the Network tab and type amazonaws into its filter box. 

Expected result: no matching requests.

What to point out: the project photographs are still stored in your private Amazon account, but the browser never talks to it and never sees a password. Doing the same on the old site shows direct Amazon traffic and two requests fetching credentials.

Needs a decision from you

Five things we could not settle ourselves

None of these block the site going live, but each is either a risk or a gap only SV Lots can close.

Rotate the Amazon storage keys

Security · do first

Those keys were readable by any visitor for as long as the old site was online, so they must be assumed known. Issue new ones and send them to us; the new site is already built to accept them, and the old compatibility path can then be deleted entirely.

Decide how enquiry messages reach you

Enquiries

The endpoint that is live on your server records a caller's name, phone, email and the service they asked about — but it has no field for the message they typed. Either your backend team adds the missing endpoint, or we switch on email delivery so the full message arrives in your inbox. Until one is chosen, you will receive the contact details but possibly not the message.

License or replace the watermarked photograph

Licensing

One image on the old site was an unlicensed Shutterstock preview with the watermark still visible across it. We have replaced it with a photograph you already own, so nothing is missing — but the old site served it publicly, which is worth resolving.

Review the two Insights articles

Content

The originals could not be reused: one described a different state entirely, and another carried a competitor's brand. We rewrote both on the same subjects, deliberately avoiding specific price or growth claims. They are published under your name, so they should have your sign-off.

Test a real staff sign-in

Access

We have no staff account, so we verified every failure case — wrong password, expired session, forged token — but not a successful login. Please sign in once with a real account. If it refuses a valid one, the server log will say exactly why.

Living with it

What is easier from here

  • Adding a project is a form your team fills in. It appears on the site and in Google's sitemap immediately.
  • Writing an article means adding one plain-text file. No developer, no deployment ceremony.
  • Changing the look happens in one place. The old site had colours written into pages roughly forty times over, in eleven different fonts; a rebrand meant editing every page and hoping nothing was missed.
  • Swapping a photograph means dropping a file in and keeping the same shape. We have documented the sizes so your team can supply drone or site photography later without any code change.
  • Knowing whether it works. The site now reports its own visitor numbers and real-world speed. The old site measured nothing, so nobody could tell.
  • Nothing was thrown away. Every original file, including the ones we removed, is preserved in the project history and can be recovered.

The one thing that would most improve the site from here is photography of your own completed layouts. Almost every wide image currently comes from either a stock library or a partner's brochure. Real aerial and site photographs of SV Lots land would raise the whole site more than any further engineering.
