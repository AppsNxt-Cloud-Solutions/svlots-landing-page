Indian real estate has historically been difficult for retail investors to assess. Two
structural changes — the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act and the arrival of
Real Estate Investment Trusts — meaningfully improved that, and both are worth
understanding before you commit capital anywhere.

## What RERA actually gives you

RERA requires developers to register qualifying projects with a state authority and
publish project details, approvals and timelines. For a buyer, the practical benefits are
narrower than the headlines suggest, but real:

- **A public record.** You can check a registered project's declared approvals and
  completion timeline rather than relying on a brochure.
- **Escrow discipline.** A defined share of collections must stay in a project-specific
  account, which constrains the historic practice of funding one project from another's
  pre-sales.
- **A complaints route.** There is a defined forum for disputes, with timelines.

The important caveat: registration is not endorsement. A RERA number tells you a project
is on the register — it does not tell you the project is well built, well located, or
fairly priced. Treat it as a starting point for diligence, not a substitute for it.

## What REITs changed

REITs let investors hold income-producing commercial property through a listed instrument,
with the rental income distributed to unit-holders. For a retail investor this separates
two things that used to be inseparable: exposure to property income, and the work of
owning a building.

That matters even if you never buy a REIT unit, because it gives you a **public benchmark**.
Listed yields and occupancy figures are a reference point for judging whether a private
opportunity is priced sensibly.

## Reading a listing after both changes

The combination shifts the questions worth asking:

1. Is the project registered, and does the registered detail match what you were told?
2. Are the approvals in the register the ones the project actually needs?
3. How does the implied yield compare to publicly listed alternatives?
4. What is the exit — who buys this from you, and on what documentation?

> Regulation reduced the range of outcomes. It did not remove the need to check the
> specifics of the asset in front of you.
